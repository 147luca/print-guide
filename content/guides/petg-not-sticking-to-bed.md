PETG is a great filament that scares people off because of the first layer. It either won't stick at all, or it sticks *too well* and tears chunks out of your bed. Both come down to a handful of settings. Here are the fixes in the order I'd actually try them.

{{< disclosure >}}

## First, the Counterintuitive Part

PETG's problem usually isn't *too little* adhesion — it's inconsistent adhesion from a first layer that's rushed or squished wrong. Fix the first layer and most "PETG won't stick" complaints vanish. Work down this list in order; stop when it sticks.

## 1. Slow the First Layer Down

This is the single biggest fix. PETG wants to be laid down calmly — try a first-layer speed around 20-25 mm/s. A rushed first layer is the #1 cause of poor PETG adhesion. If you change one thing, change this.

## 2. Raise the Bed Temperature

PETG likes a warmer bed than PLA. Start around 75-80°C and adjust. Too cool and the first layer never grabs; the right temp lets it settle and bond.

## 3. Get the Z-Offset Right

PETG is fussier about first-layer height than PLA. You want the lines slightly squished into the plate — not floating (won't stick) and not so crushed the nozzle drags (causes the opposite problem). Re-run a Z-offset calibration specifically for PETG; the value that's perfect for PLA is often a touch too high for PETG.

## 4. Clean the Bed (Really Clean It)

Finger oils kill adhesion. Wash the plate with warm water and dish soap, or wipe with isopropyl alcohol. Do this more often than you think — a bed that worked yesterday can fail today from one handprint.

## 5. Match the Surface to PETG

- **Textured PEI:** great for PETG — strong but releases cleanly once cool.
- **Smooth PEI:** PETG can bond *too* hard and tear the sheet. Use a release agent here (a thin glue-stick layer doubles as a barrier so PETG doesn't weld to the plate).
- **Glass:** needs glue stick or hairspray as a barrier.

The glue stick on smooth surfaces isn't for *more* grip — it's a barrier so PETG doesn't fuse to the bed and rip it.

## 6. Kill Drafts and Cooling

PETG hates aggressive part cooling on the first layers. Drop the cooling fan to 0-30% for the first few layers, and keep cold air off the bed. A draft across the plate causes uneven cooling and corner lifting.

## 7. Check If the Filament Is Wet

If you've done all the above and still get poor, bubbly, or popping first layers, the filament may be wet — PETG absorbs moisture and prints rough when it does. See [how to dry filament](/guides/how-to-dry-filament/) to rule it out.

## The Order That Usually Works

For most people the fix is: **slow first layer + correct bed temp + clean bed + dialed Z-offset.** That combination solves the large majority of PETG adhesion problems. Add a release barrier if you're on smooth PEI or glass, and manage cooling/drafts for tall prints.

## Bottom Line

PETG isn't hard — it's just less forgiving than PLA on the first layer. Slow it down, warm the bed, clean the surface, and dial the Z-offset. Once it's tuned it's one of the most useful filaments you can run.
