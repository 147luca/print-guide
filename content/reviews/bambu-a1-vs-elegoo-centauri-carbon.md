These are the two budget printers people actually cross-shop in 2026. Both are excellent. They win on different things, so the right answer depends on what you value. Here's the honest breakdown.

{{< disclosure >}}

## The Verdict First

- **Buy the Bambu Lab A1** if you want the smoothest, most hands-off experience and a polished software ecosystem.
- **Buy the Elegoo Centauri Carbon** if you want the best print quality per dollar and don't mind a louder machine and a bit more tuning.

Neither is a bad choice. This is a "match it to your priority" decision, not a "one is clearly better" decision.

## Print Quality

**Centauri Carbon wins, narrowly.** Out of the box at 0.2mm with tuned settings, the Centauri produces surface finish that rivals machines costing significantly more. We've run it daily for 6 months across PLA, PETG, TPU, PLA-CF, and ABS ([full review here](/reviews/elegoo-centauri-carbon-review/)).

The A1 is no slouch — its quality is genuinely good — but the Centauri has a slight edge on fine detail and surface consistency once both are dialed in.

**Edge: Elegoo Centauri Carbon.**

## Ease of Use

**Bambu A1 wins, clearly.** This is Bambu's whole thing. The calibration routine handles flow, vibration compensation, and bed mesh automatically. You unbox it and you're printing usable parts within an hour with almost no decisions to make.

The Centauri requires a tuning pass per filament type to get its best results. Not hard, but it's effort the A1 mostly removes.

**Edge: Bambu Lab A1.**

## Noise

**Bambu A1 wins.** The A1 is quiet enough to run in a bedroom or office. The Centauri is noticeably louder — Elegoo's marketing understates this. If the printer will live in a shared or quiet space, this matters a lot.

If it lives in a garage or workshop, it's a non-issue.

**Edge: Bambu Lab A1.**

## Software & Ecosystem

**Bambu A1 wins.** Bambu Studio and the cloud/handheld integration are the smoothest in the price range. Sending prints, monitoring, and managing the machine is frictionless.

The tradeoff: Bambu's ecosystem is somewhat walled. Third-party filament works, but the smoothest path is theirs, and it's cloud-connected by default (local-only mode exists but is less polished).

The Centauri uses OrcaSlicer, which is excellent and open, but the overall integration is less seamless.

**Edge: Bambu Lab A1** (with an asterisk if you value openness over polish — then the Centauri's OrcaSlicer path appeals).

## Filament Flexibility

**Roughly even.** Both handle the common range — PLA, PETG, TPU, carbon-fiber blends, and ABS (with an enclosure for ABS on either). The Centauri's tuning-per-material requirement is more pronounced but yields slightly better results once done.

**Edge: tie.**

## Price & Value

Both sit in the sub-$400 budget tier and trade places on sales. Dollar-for-dollar:
- The **A1** gives you more *convenience* per dollar.
- The **Centauri** gives you more *print quality* per dollar.

**Edge: depends on what you're optimizing.**

## Who Should Buy Which

**Buy the Bambu Lab A1 if:**
- This is your first printer.
- The printer will live in a quiet/shared space.
- You value your time over saving a tuning session.
- You want the smoothest software experience.

**Buy the Elegoo Centauri Carbon if:**
- Print quality is your top priority.
- Noise isn't a concern (garage, workshop).
- You enjoy dialing in settings, or at least don't mind it.
- You prefer open software (OrcaSlicer) over a walled ecosystem.

## Bottom Line

The A1 is the better *appliance*; the Centauri is the better *tool*. If you want to press print and get great results with zero fuss, the A1. If you want the best-looking parts per dollar and you're willing to tune, the Centauri. Both are genuinely good — you're choosing your priority, not avoiding a lemon.
