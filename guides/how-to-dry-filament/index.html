How to Dry Filament: Signs It's Wet and How to Fix It

Wet filament ruins prints in ways that look like printer problems. How to tell if your filament is wet, how to dry it properly, and how to keep it dry.
May 21, 2026 · 3 min · Luca

Half the “my printer is broken” problems I’ve chased turned out to be wet filament. It mimics real hardware faults — popping sounds, stringing, rough surfaces, weak parts — so people tear down a perfectly good machine when the fix was a few hours in a dryer.

Here’s how to tell, and what to do.

Signs Your Filament Is Wet#

Wet filament gives itself away. Watch and listen for:

  • Popping or crackling from the nozzle while printing — that’s trapped moisture flashing to steam.
  • Stringing and oozing that no retraction tuning fixes. If you’ve dialed retraction and it still strings, suspect moisture.
  • Rough, fuzzy, or inconsistent surfaces where it used to print smooth.
  • Weak, brittle parts that snap along layer lines — steam disrupts layer bonding.
  • Visible steam or a “fizzy” extrusion in bad cases.

The tell is when a filament that used to print fine suddenly prints badly. The spool didn’t change — its moisture content did.

Which Filaments Absorb Water Fastest#

Not all filament is equally thirsty. Roughly worst to best:

  • Nylon / PA — drinks water out of the air. Almost always needs drying.
  • PLA-CF and other carbon/glass-filled blends — the fibers wick moisture fast. Mine prints rough and popping within days of being left out.
  • TPU — flexible filaments are moisture-sensitive.
  • PETG — moderate; noticeable when humid.
  • PLA — most forgiving, but still degrades over months in a humid room.

If you live somewhere humid, even PLA benefits from storage discipline.

How to Dry Filament#

Two realistic options:

A dedicated filament dryer (best). A filament dryer box holds a set temperature and many let you print straight from the box. This is the set-and-forget answer and what I use for CF blends.

  • PLA: ~45-50°C for 4-6 hours
  • PETG: ~55-65°C for 4-6 hours
  • TPU: ~50°C for 4-6 hours
  • PLA-CF / Nylon: ~70°C for 6-8+ hours

Your oven (works in a pinch, with caution). Lowest setting, ideally with an oven thermometer because home ovens overshoot. Stay near the glass-transition temp — too hot and the spool fuses into a useless brick. Honestly, a dryer is cheaper than the filament you’ll ruin learning your oven’s true temperature.

How to Keep It Dry#

Drying is treating the symptom. Storage is the cure:

  • Airtight container + desiccant between prints. Cheap and effective.
  • Print from a dry box for the thirsty filaments (CF, nylon, TPU).
  • Don’t leave spools on the printer for weeks. That’s how “it printed fine last month” becomes today’s failed print.

Bottom Line#

Before you blame the printer, blame the spool. Wet filament is the most common invisible cause of bad prints, and it’s the easiest to fix. Dry it, store it sealed with desiccant, and a whole category of “printer problems” disappears.
